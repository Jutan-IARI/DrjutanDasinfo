About

My work sits between machine learning and genome biology. Most genomes are mostly non-coding, and most of that sequence has no assigned function — my research builds computational methods that assign it one, and that predict what happens when we edit it.

Three threads run through the work: discovering long non-coding RNAs in fish species where no annotation exists, predicting CRISPR-Cas9 off-target activity in crop genomes, and building open web resources so that biologists without programming backgrounds can use both. I completed my Ph.D. at ICAR-IARI, New Delhi in 2025 and now teach and supervise at Kalinga University.

I grew up in a farming family in Sepahijala district, Tripura, which is still why agricultural genomes are the ones I care most about getting right.

Current focus

Deep learning for CRISPR-Cas9 guide design and off-target prediction in cereal crops; extending fish lncRNA discovery to aquaculture species of economic importance in Northeast India.

Research

Method development, applied to genomes where annotation is thin and the stakes are agricultural.

Non-coding RNA discovery in fish genomes

Long non-coding RNAs regulate development, stress response and immunity, but fish genomes are poorly annotated for them and models trained on mammalian data transfer badly. My doctoral work produced a fish-specific prediction framework and an accompanying genomic web resource, built on sequence and structural features rather than borrowed mammalian priors.

CRISPR-Cas9 off-target prediction in crops

Off-target cleavage is the main barrier to regulatory acceptance of edited crops. Beginning with my M.Sc. work on cleavage-site estimation across 15 crop species, this thread has moved from classical machine learning toward deep architectures that learn guide–target mismatch tolerance directly from sequence.

Genomic web resources

Prediction methods only matter if wet-lab groups can run them. Every project in my lab ships as a public web server or database alongside the paper.
